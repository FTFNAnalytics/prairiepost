# Network expansion — the name slate

**Status (23 Nov 2026):** twelve names chosen by the owner for the regions
the network doesn't cover yet. **Nothing is registered and nothing is
built.** This file is the record.

**28 Nov 2026:** five of the twelve are built as unbranded foundations on
Toronto Telegraph's design, the owner's choice of the network's cleanest:
Citadel Courier, The Easterly, Le Memphré, Les Chutes and Borealis
Bulletin. Each waited on its brand package (`docs/brand-packages.md` says
what one carries). Four arrived and were applied the same day; Borealis
Bulletin's has not. Their domains were still unregistered on 28 Nov, and
nothing is registered yet. An earlier brainstorm lived only in a
chat and was lost when that chat was compacted, so the slate, the reasons
behind it and the rejected names all live here now.

## Where the network has no paper

- **The North** (Yukon, Northwest Territories, Nunavut): none. Turtle
  Island Times covers Indigenous affairs nationally; its "territories" are
  Indigenous territories, not the North.
- **Atlantic Canada:** none.
- **Quebec:** Le Bleuet Blanc already covers the whole province in French
  from Saguenay ("seventeen regions, one by one"), and the Rideau Review
  covers Gatineau. New Quebec titles are therefore city and regional papers.
- **Saskatchewan:** no paper. Western Wire carries Saskatchewan only as a
  wire region.

### Domains

Every domain in the table was **unregistered on 2026-11-23 at 00:16 UTC**,
checked against the registries themselves over RDAP (CIRA for .ca,
Verisign for .com). Unregistered is not reserved: nothing is held until
it is bought.

The network's domains drop the leading article (bisonbulletin.ca,
bleuetblanc.ca), so the .ca column does too. Where the article-free .com
was already taken, the .com column uses the article form instead. Other
forms that were free at the same check:

- Matching pairs with the article: theeasterly.ca, lestrien.ca,
  lesrapides.ca, lememphre.ca, letrifluvien.ca and letrifluvien.com.
- **leschutes.com**, the exact masthead. Worth holding; leschutes.ca and
  chutes.ca are taken. leschutesquebec.ca and leschutesquebec.com were
  free too.
- stjohnsignal.ca and stjohnsignal.com, the common typo of the St. John's
  domain. Worth holding.
- saintjohnsignal.ca and saintjohnsignal.com. Careful: **Saint John is a
  different city** (New Brunswick). The Signal belongs to St. John's,
  Newfoundland, and its masthead keeps the apostrophe-s.

## Why each name

- **Borealis Bulletin:** the northern lights are visible across all three
  territories, and "boreal" also names the forest. It would be the
  network's fourth Bulletin (Brampton, Bison, Brandon); the owner chose it
  over the proposed Borealis Beacon, whose domains were also free on
  22 Nov. About half of northerners are Indigenous, so the northern
  candidates deliberately stayed with sky and land rather than borrowing
  Inuit or Dene symbols.
- **The Easterly:** an east wind off the Atlantic. It is the region-wide
  umbrella, the way Western Wire covers the West, and it breaks the
  alliteration pattern the way Civis Media and Le Bleuet Blanc do.
- **Citadel Courier:** the Halifax Citadel, the city's landmark.
- **St. John's Signal:** Signal Hill is where Marconi received the first
  transatlantic wireless signal in 1901. *The Signal* was also the paper
  locked-out Evening Telegram staff published during the 1979 strike.
  Caution: King's College's journalism school in Halifax publishes a news
  site called The Signal.
- **Hub City Herald:** Moncton's nickname, from its history as the
  Maritimes' rail hub.
- **Les Chutes:** Montmorency Falls, 83 m high, about 30 m taller than
  Niagara. Plural on purpose: the singular *La Chute* sounds exactly like
  Lachute, a town in the Laurentians, and *Le Chutes* isn't French.
- **Les Rapides:** the Lachine Rapids stopped ships from going farther
  upriver, so Montreal grew where cargo came ashore. *Rapide* also means
  quick.
- **L'Estrien:** named for its readers, the French tradition behind
  *Le Parisien*. The incumbent daily is La Tribune.
- **Le Memphré:** Lake Memphrémagog's legendary monster; the playful
  title on the slate. It shares Estrie with L'Estrien (see Still open).
- **Le Trifluvien:** named for the readers of Trois-Rivières. It revives a
  title: a Catholic paper called *Le Trifluvien* ran from about 1888 to
  1906. Falls, rapids and "three rivers": Les Chutes, Les Rapides and
  Le Trifluvien read as a family.
- **Living Skies Ledger:** "Land of Living Skies" is on Saskatchewan's
  licence plates. Province-wide, the way the Bison Bulletin covers
  Manitoba.
- **Regina Record:** the Regina city paper under the Ledger. The incumbent
  daily is the Leader-Post.

## Still open

- **Prince Edward Island:** no name chosen. Proposed: Charlottetown
  Clarion (charlottetownclarion.ca and .com free on 22 Nov), with Island
  Inquirer as the alternate.
- **Estrie:** L'Estrien and Le Memphré cover the same region. Either
  Le Memphré is the Magog and lake-country local under L'Estrien's
  regional umbrella, or one is the other's alternate. Undecided.
  On 28 Nov the owner's brand guidelines gave Le Memphré the whole
  region, Sherbrooke and the Eastern Townships, and its brand build
  follows them. Whether L'Estrien is still wanted is the owner's
  question (`docs/brand-packages.md`).
- **A second Saskatchewan city:** Saskatoon Sentinel was free on 22 Nov.
- **English-language Montreal:** The Montreal Mercury
  (montrealmercury.ca and .com free on 22 Nov), if one is wanted. The
  Gazette is the city's only English daily; the name nods to the Quebec
  Mercury, Quebec City's English paper from 1805 to 1903.

## Considered and set aside

Recorded so nobody re-checks them. "Taken" means the domain was already
registered when checked on 22 or 23 Nov.

**The North**
- Borealis Beacon: the proposed name; free, set aside for Bulletin.
- Taiga Times: taiga describes the southern territories; Nunavut is tundra.
- The Parallel Post: all three territories begin at the 60th parallel,
  but the name needs explaining.
- Tundra Tribune: too close to the *Tundra Times*, the historic Alaska
  Native newspaper.
- Anything with "True North": a national partisan outlet uses it.
- Polar Post, Northern Ledger, Sixty North: taken.

**Atlantic Canada**
- Tideline Times: the region-wide alternate; free.
- Atlantic Anchor: taken. Anything starting "Salt": SaltWire is the
  region's dominant newspaper chain.
- Chebucto Chronicle, Halifax Harbinger: Halifax alternates; free.
  Chebucto is the anglicized Mi'kmaw name for the harbour, in everyday
  use but hard for outsiders to pronounce.
- Halifax Herald: the Chronicle Herald's own historic name.
- Outport Observer: the alternate for a rural Newfoundland and Labrador
  paper; free. Cabot Courier and Avalon Advocate: free, unused.
- Fundy Free Press: the province-wide New Brunswick alternate; free.
  Tidal Times and Fundy Forum: .ca free, .com taken.

**Quebec**
- Alternates, all free: Le Promontoire (Quebec City, lepromontoire.ca),
  La Traverse (the Québec–Lévis ferry, latraversequebec.ca), L'Archipel
  (Montreal sits on the Hochelaga Archipelago, archipelmontreal.ca),
  Le Beauceron, Le Rimouskois, Le Lavallois, La Batture, Le Couchant,
  La Drave, Le Grand Large, L'Harricana. Rimouski already has *Le Soir*;
  Laval has *Courrier Laval* and *L'Écho de Laval*.
- Le Mont-Royal: Les Amis de la montagne publish at lemontroyal.qc.ca,
  and there is also the Town of Mount Royal.
- Ville-Marie: also a town in Témiscamingue, with its own paper.
- Le Montréalais, La Métropole: taken.
- Le Fleuve: the name of a co-op daily in the Lower St. Lawrence in the
  1990s.
- Townships Times: too close to the *Townships Sun* and *Townships
  Weekend*.
- Le Harfang: a Quebec nationalist review has published under that name,
  and Beauport's old junior hockey team was the Harfangs.
- La Chute: sounds exactly like Lachute. Les Remparts: the junior hockey
  team. Le Diamant: Robert Lepage's theatre.
- Bare-word domains taken: Le Fjord, Le Rocher, Le Phare, La Marée,
  Le Béluga, Le Rang, Le Perron, Les Forges, Les Cantons, Le Gaspésien.
  Any could still work with the place added to the domain, as with
  chutesquebec.ca.

**Saskatchewan**
- Grid Road Gazette: the rural-first alternate; free.
- Sask Sentinel, Saskatoon Sentinel: free.
- Avoided: Star, Phoenix, Leader and Post (the two Postmedia dailies),
  "Prairie" (the flagship's word) and "Wheat City" (Brandon's nickname).

## The house style this slate follows

- English: an alliterative place or icon plus a classic newspaper word
  (Bison Bulletin, Rideau Review).
- French: a regional icon (Le Bleuet Blanc) or the readers' own name for
  themselves (the *Le Parisien* model).
- No sound-alikes with papers already in the network (Caribou would
  collide with the Cariboo Compass).
- No name an established outlet already uses. A clean web search is not
  trademark clearance, though.
- Domains drop the leading article.

## From name to launch

1. Register both domains for each masthead you keep.
2. Run a trademark search with the Canadian Intellectual Property Office
   on each final masthead.
3. Brand package from the owner, then the brand build:
   `php tools/make-paper.php <slug> <template> "<Name>"` scaffolds the
   files, and the launch pack gets its six-story inaugural edition (about
   the paper itself, as CLAUDE.md describes) at brand-build time, never
   earlier. Since 28 Nov the owner wants new papers on Toronto Telegraph's
   design with their own branding and colours; the five foundations above
   are that design, with every colour in one block of role variables.
4. French titles launch on the i18n layer Le Bleuet Blanc introduced
   (`chrome.lang` set to `fr` in `palette.json`); see
   DEPLOY-BLEUETBLANC.md.
5. Check each paper against the compliance gate in PLAN.md and the launch
   lessons in CLAUDE.md, and give it its own `DEPLOY-<NAME>.md`.
6. DNS to the VPS, then the paper's launch runbook.
